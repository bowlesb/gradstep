Privacy

Private beta · last updated 16 October 2026

Gradstep is a reader for machine-learning papers on iPhone, iPad and Mac, and in a web browser. This page explains what the apps and our servers handle during the private beta, who processes it, and the choices you have.

Papers and PDFs

Your device fetches a paper’s official HTML version from arXiv and prepares it for reading on the device. Our servers don’t fetch, store or convert it.

A PDF you add, choose, drop or download is opened and kept only on your device. It is never uploaded to our servers. In a browser, a PDF stays in the file you chose and the tab it is open in. An experimental option in the apps prepares a PDF with models that run on the device itself.

Our servers never contact arXiv; your device does, as you read. When you add a paper, our servers look up its catalog details (title, authors, abstract, version and licence) from DataCite, which holds arXiv’s records, and from Crossref; for a paper that isn’t on arXiv, from OpenAlex. Your device also sends the details it read from the paper’s page; they stay with your own library. When you add a paper from a reference in another paper, or ask chat to find one, the reference’s text is sent to Crossref, OpenAlex and DataCite to identify it. Paper search runs from your device: the apps send what you type to arXiv and DataCite, and a browser sends it to DataCite, without passing it through our servers. (Earlier app versions send it to our servers, which ask DataCite.) The Feed’s list of new papers comes from DataCite’s records of arXiv papers, which our servers read once for everyone.

Our servers keep no copies of papers: no PDFs, conversions or reading copies. Copies made on our servers before 13 October 2026 were deleted on 16 October 2026. The only paper text our servers hold is what your device sends to make an episode, deleted once the episode is made.

Your library

Your library (which papers you have added, which you archived and the tags you give them) is kept on our servers so it syncs between your devices. Our service can read it: it is not encrypted with your device keys the way your conversations are.

To suggest topic tags for a paper, its title and abstract (public information about the paper, nothing about you) are sent to Anthropic. The Feed’s “For you” list is ranked on our servers from the topics in your library, without an AI model.

When you ask for recommended papers, the paper’s title, authors and abstract (for a catalog, those of up to eight of its papers, never its name) and the public details of candidate papers found in DataCite and the Feed are sent to Anthropic’s Claude through OpenRouter, which chooses among them. The papers added and the one-line reasons are kept with your library on our servers until you delete your account.

We also keep the records needed to run the service: your devices, which days you used Gradstep and which parts of it (for example the library, sync, chat or the Feed), usage and credits. Daily activity records are kept for up to 400 days.

Conversations and notes

Your conversations, their titles, your notes, reading positions, and the diagrams and figure breakdowns in answers are encrypted on your device, with keys kept on your devices, before they are stored or synced. Our servers store only the encrypted form. In a browser, the keys are kept by that browser.

A new device is approved from a device you are already signed in on, or with your recovery code. If you lose every device and your recovery code, we cannot recover that content.

AI answers

When you ask a question, the app sends your question, any passage you selected, earlier messages in that conversation and, for questions about a paper, the paper’s text to the model selected in Answer options: Anthropic for Claude, DeepSeek for DeepSeek, or Google through OpenRouter for Gemini Flash. When you ask about a figure or table, its image from the paper goes too. Anthropic also writes conversation titles and suggested questions. Equation and figure explanations use your selected chat model.

The equation view sends the equation and the paper’s text in the same way. Study mode also sends its record of what you have covered so far, and a Claude model updates that record from your reply and the tutor’s answer. A Claude model writes each conversation’s title from your question, and suggests follow-up questions from your question and the start of the answer.

When a question needs the web, the searches the model writes go to Tavily or, for Claude, Anthropic’s search provider as a backup. Search services see the searches, which can include words from your question or paper.

Our servers pass these requests through without storing their text. The app asks for consent before cloud AI use and again when the consent changes to name a new processor. Google through OpenRouter, Anthropic, DeepSeek and the search services process requests under their own terms and may retain them; we do not promise zero retention. Answers are saved, encrypted, on your device.

Episodes

To make an episode of a paper you have opened, your device sends the paper’s text to our servers, where it is kept privately for your account only while the episode is being made. The writer you choose, Anthropic’s Claude or DeepSeek, writes and checks the script from that text. Google Vertex AI turns the script into voices. These providers process the text under their own terms.

We delete the paper’s text as soon as the episode is ready, or once it can’t be made or you cancel it, usually within minutes. Text sent for an episode you didn’t go on to make is deleted after a day. We never show that text back in the app.

Your episode’s audio and transcript are stored privately for your account, and only your account can play them. Links to the audio expire within 15 minutes. Your episodes are kept until you delete your account. Shared episodes in the catalog are made only from papers under CC BY or CC0 licences.

Your account

You sign in with Apple, Google or an email code. We keep your email address and the identifiers from these sign-in services to run your account. Sign-in codes are sent by email through Resend. During the beta, new accounts are by invitation.

Purchases and credits

Plans and credit packs are sold through the App Store. Apple processes payment; we receive transaction details to add credits to your account and keep a record of credit use.

Reports and support

If you send a report from the app, we store its text and any screenshots you attach, privately, to investigate it. Reports are visible to our support staff, who sign in with two-factor authentication. Reports are kept until you delete your account.

What we don’t do

  • We don’t sell your data or use it for advertising.
  • The apps contain no advertising or third-party analytics code.
  • We don’t train AI models on your data.

Where data is processed

  • Our database, file storage and server functions run on Supabase in the United States (US West, Oregon).
  • Paper lookups, topic tags and episode production run on Google Cloud in the United States (US West, Oregon). Episode voices use Google Vertex AI’s global service.
  • This website and the browser version of Gradstep are served by Cloudflare.
  • Google through OpenRouter, Anthropic, DeepSeek and Tavily process the requests described above under their own terms. AI requests are not restricted to our servers’ region.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app or in the browser: on iPhone, iPad or Mac, Settings → Account → Delete account; in the browser, the account menu → Delete account. The device or browser you delete from also removes its own copy and keys, and your other devices are signed out. We then delete your library, your encrypted history, your devices and keys, your private papers and episodes (and any paper text still waiting for an episode), your reports, your activity records and your sign-in details. Records of paid work, such as credit and usage totals and the episodes you paid for, are kept linked only to an internal number, no longer to your email or sign-in. Catalog episodes that other readers use remain.

Backups

Our database is backed up daily and each backup is kept for 7 days, so data you delete leaves our backups within a week.
