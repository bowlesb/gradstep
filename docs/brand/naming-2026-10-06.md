# Naming study (2026-10-06)

> **Decided 2026-10-06: Gradstep** ([decisions](../DECISIONS.md#product-name-gradstep-owner-decided-2026-10-06)).

Independent research agent; owner request "audit the name choice of TimeStep … list options … research what else is out there". Checks are as of 2026-10-06 and are not legal clearance.


**Short version:** stop using TimeStep as the public name. Someone else runs a live AI chat product on timestep.ai and timestep.com. My top pick is **Gradstep**, with **Glossator** if you want a name that is easier to own and **Paperstep** as the option that needs the least rework.

I checked everything on 2026-10-06. "Verified" means I looked it up myself:
- **App Store:** the US store, through Apple's iTunes Search API.
- **Domains:** the domain registries, through WHOIS and RDAP. I was able to run these, so domain status is registry data, not a guess. "Unregistered" means the registry has no record of the name. Some unregistered names still carry premium prices.
- **Websites:** I fetched the live homepages.
- **Trademarks:** USPTO records, through the [Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com) mirror. I don't know how up to date that mirror is.

**Not checked:** the official USPTO search tool, EU, UK and WIPO trademark registers, unregistered prior use, X, Instagram and Bluesky handles, and App Stores outside the US.

## 1. Verdict on the current names

**TimeStep: stop using it as the public name, but keep it as the internal codename.**

- **Meaning.** ML people will get the joke: the diffusion step *t*, an RL environment step, an ODE solver step. Everyone else will read it as a timer or time-tracker. Nothing in it says papers, reading or AI.
- **An AI product already uses the name.** [timestep.ai](https://timestep.ai) is live. Its site description reads: *"Timestep is a personal assistant and memory control plane you can chat with and connect to over MCP."*
  - timestep.com (registered 2002) redirects to it; timestep.ai was registered in 2021.
  - An AI chat product holding both the .com and the .ai is the worst possible clash for an AI chat app.
  - I found no reference to it in the science-reader or textbrain repos. If it turns out to be yours, this point goes away.
- **App Store:** there is already an app called ["Timestep"](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/timestep/id6755438810), a time tracker by Sebastian Schoen (version 1.2.1, May 2026).
- **Other domains and handles:** timestep.app is registered and serves a Brazilian timesheet product, "Timesheet STEP". The GitHub username `timestep` is taken.
- **Trademark:** TIMESTEP is live and registered to Stagestep, Inc. for vinyl dance flooring (class 19, serial 97533616). That is a different class, so the direct risk is low. "T TIMESTEP" in class 9 is cancelled. Sources: [Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/search/trademarks?q=timestep), [Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/746/02/t-timestep-74602291.html).
- **Search.** You won't be found. A search for "timestep.ai" returns TensorFlow Agents, Unreal Engine and Phaser developer docs.

**Paperstep: available and low-risk, but generic.**
- The App Store has [PaperSteps](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/papersteps/id1499207340), a hand-drawn pedometer from 2020. It's a different category but almost the same name.
- Trademarkia shows 0 results.
- paperstep.com was registered in 2017 and is parked with a hosting company. paperstep.app, paperstep.ai, paperstepapp.com, getpaperstep.com and the GitHub handle are all free.
- "Paper-" is the most crowded prefix in this category (see section 2).

**Science Reader: keep it for the codebase only.**
- It describes the product rather than naming it. My understanding is that descriptive names get weak trademark protection, but a trademark lawyer should confirm that.
- [sciencereader.com](https://sciencereader.com) is a live site titled "AI Research and Popular Science Stories".
- Children's-book trademarks such as "Delta Science Readers" are live in class 16.
- It doesn't say "ML". sciencereader.app and sciencereader.ai are unregistered.

## 2. The landscape

**Live products I confirmed by loading their homepages.** Scholarcy and SciSpace only returned bot-check pages, but they did respond.
- **alphaXiv**, which also has an [iOS app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/alphaxiv/id6782411985) with 17 ratings.
- **AI research search and review:** Elicit, Consensus, Scholarcy, SciSpace (typeset.io now redirects there), Semantic Reader and Asta (both from Ai2), Paperguide, Undermind, Anara.
- **Explaining and summarising papers:** Explainpaper, Paper Digest, Moonlight, Emergent Mind.
- **Chatting with PDFs:** Humata, ChatDOC, ChatPaper.
- **Finding papers through citations:** Connected Papers, ResearchRabbit, Litmaps.
- **Reference managers and readers:** Zotero, Papers by ReadCube, Paperpile, Readwise Reader.
- **Paper feeds:** Scholar Inbox, Cool Papers, Hugging Face Daily Papers. paperswithcode.com now redirects to Hugging Face.
- **Name changes:** notebooklm.google now redirects to a page titled "Gemini Notebook", and afforai.com now redirects to Logically.

**Direct iOS competitors (US App Store search):**
- arXivChat: AI-Era Paper Reader
- Shua: arXiv Paper Reader
- Reader for arXiv
- Read arXiv
- Lib arXiv
- FirstPass for arXiv
- Persimmon: Research Simplified
- Aprecis – Read AI Papers
- ZiNote
- PaperCrush
- Scollr

None of them has more than 17 US ratings. Nobody owns this niche yet, but it is full of names that sound alike.

**Overused naming patterns:**
- **"Paper" or "Papers":** Paperpile, Paperguide, Paper Digest, Connected Papers, Papers, PaperCrush, Paperly, ChatPaper and Explainpaper. Also [Paperspace](https://www.paperspace.com), a well-known GPU cloud for ML.
- **"Scholar" or "Sci-":** Semantic Scholar, Scholarcy, ScholarAI, Scholar Inbox, SciSpace, Scite.
- **"arXiv" or "-Xiv":** [arXiv's brand guidelines](https://info.arxiv.org/brand/brand-guidelines.html) prohibit using "arXiv" in the name of a non-arXiv project in a way that implies an official connection.
- **"Research-", "Chat-" and "-AI":** ResearchRabbit, ResearchGate, ResearchPod, ChatDOC, ChatPaper.
- **Famous ML terms are already taken:**
  - [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai), an AI research organisation.
  - [Argmax](https://www.argmaxinc.com), which builds on-device AI models, the same platform you're on.
  - [Softmax](https://softmax.com), an AI alignment company.
  - [Distill](https://distill.pub), a well-known ML journal.
  - [Nabla](https://nabla.com), a medical AI company with iOS apps.

The leading products each use a single coined or repurposed word with a strong feel: Elicit, Consensus, Undermind, Moonlight, Zotero.

## 3. Candidates

**How to read the tables:**
- **Scores** are 1–5 for Distinctiveness / Fit / Pronounceability / Searchability (D/F/P/S).
- **Domains:** ✓ means the registry has no record of the name; ✗ means it's registered.
- **TM** is the Trademarkia search result.
- **App Store** results are from the US store.

Every ordinary English word I tested has its .com, .app and .ai all registered. That includes Readout, Margin, Gloss, Lookahead, Unroll, Denoise, Prior, Saliency, Lemma, Peruse, Reflow, Lectern, Sidenote and Scholia. A name you can actually own will have to be a compound or a rare word. You'd pair it with a "get-" or "-app" .com, or with the .app domain.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Gradstep: the best balance.**
   - It's an ML term without sounding like an API function, and it keeps the "step" story from TimeStep.
   - It's easy to say and spell, and at 8 characters it fits under the icon.
   - I found no App Store or USPTO matches, and the .com has a known price.
   - Suggested App Store name: "Gradstep: ML Paper Reader".
   - Risk: "grad" may read as students only. A subtitle such as "for researchers and engineers" can widen it.
2. **Glossator: the most distinctive and the easiest to own.**
   - It is the closest fit in meaning for an AI that explains papers.
   - Choose it if you want a brand rather than a pun.
   - The cost is that people have to learn to spell and say it ("GLOSS-ay-ter").
3. **Paperstep: the least work.**
   - It is already the App Store Connect record, its trademark search came back clean, and the .app is free.
   - But it is generic and sits in the most crowded naming pattern.

Runner-up: **Readpass**, if the paywall/password sound doesn't bother you.

**Next steps to secure a name**

1. **Trademark.** Pick two finalists and have a trademark attorney run a quick screening search, then a full search.
   - Classes to cover: 9 (downloadable software), 42 (software as a service) and 41 (education).
   - The search should include similar-sounding marks and unregistered use, plus the EU and UK if those are likely markets.
   - Then file an "intent-to-use" application on the winner.
   - My Trademarkia checks only catch near-exact matches in a third-party mirror. They are not legal clearance.
2. **App Store.**
   - Once you decide, rename the existing "Paperstep" record (App Information → Name).
   - Its uploaded TestFlight builds should keep the record active. Apple's old rule of 120 or 180 days to hold a name without a build is [inconsistently documented](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/706597).
   - The bundle ID can't be changed, but users never see it, so the internal "timestep" and "paperstep" identifiers can stay.
   - Keep the home-screen name to 12 characters or fewer.
3. **Domains.**
   - Register the free .app and .ai, plus a "get-" or "-app" .com. They're cheap and you can drop them later.
   - Buying gradstep.com for $1,995 is a one-off cost that needs your sign-off. It is separate from the $100/month operating budget.
4. **Social handles.**
   - Claim the name on GitHub, X, Bluesky, Instagram, Threads, YouTube and LinkedIn.
   - I only checked GitHub, where gradstep, paperstep and readpass are free. Use namechk.com or check the other platforms by hand.
5. **Before announcing.**
   - Check App Stores outside the US.
   - Confirm whether timestep.ai belongs to anyone you know.
